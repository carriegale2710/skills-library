# Reference sources

The methods in ux-researcher are checked against these practitioner sources. When a skill and a source disagree, the source wins; update the skill.

## Process frameworks

- **IDEO.org, The Field Guide to Human-Centered Design** (2015): 57 methods across Inspiration, Ideation, Implementation; the desirable, feasible, viable lenses. Methods online at https://www.designkit.org/methods
  - Brainstorm Rules: https://www.designkit.org/methods/brainstorm-rules.html
  - Co-Creation Session: https://www.designkit.org/methods/co-creation-session.html
- **Stanford d.school**: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test; Point of View and How Might We. https://dschool.stanford.edu/resources
- **Design Council, the double diamond**: Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver. https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/the-double-diamond/
- **GV Design Sprint**: map, sketch (Crazy 8s), decide, storyboard, prototype, test with five users. https://www.gv.com/sprint/
- **IBM Enterprise Design Thinking**: Hills, Playbacks, Sponsor Users. https://www.ibm.com/design/thinking/

## Research methods (Nielsen Norman Group)

- When to Use Which UX Research Methods (attitudinal vs. behavioral, qualitative vs. quantitative, context of use; methods by phase): https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/
- 10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/
- Severity Ratings for Usability Problems (0 to 4 scale; average three or more evaluators): https://www.nngroup.com/papers/heuristic/severityrating.html
- Persona types (proto, qualitative, statistical): https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persona-types/
- Topic hubs for interviews, journey mapping, empathy mapping, diary studies, card sorting, tree testing, and usability testing: https://www.nngroup.com/topic/research-methods/

## Other

- Jobs to be Done: Clayton Christensen, *Competing Against Luck* (2016); Anthony Ulwick, *Jobs to be Done: Theory to Practice* (2016).
- Lean UX and assumption mapping: Jeff Gothelf and Josh Seiden, *Lean UX* (3rd ed., 2021).
