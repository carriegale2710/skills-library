# Research Prompts and Sources

Purpose: source types and prompt patterns for `find-wedge` step 3. Replace `[ ]` with real values.

## Source types (use 4+)

Reddit/forum threads; G2/Capterra/app-store reviews; Notion, Zapier, Make, Google Sheets templates; YouTube workflow tutorials; job posts; search demand (problem-shaped queries, not brand queries).

## Prompts

- Find 30 recent discussions where [buyer] describes difficulty with [job]. Extract workarounds, tools, costs, switching language.
- For [competitor], list recurring 1-3 star complaints, feature requests, implementation complaints. Separate one-offs from patterns.
- Find templates/artefacts people use for [job]. Identify manual steps and integrations.
- Is demand for [problem] rising, stable, or declining?
- Map the top 5 incumbents for [job]: buyer, pricing, onboarding burden, integrations, structural constraints.
- Generate 5 wedge candidates for [buyer] and [job]; reject any lacking buyer, trigger, incumbent constraint, or expansion path.
- Design the cheapest test for [wedge]: audience, channel, offer, metric, sample size, kill criteria.
- Challenge [wedge]: what keeps a buyer with the incumbent; how easily can the incumbent copy it?
- Apply kill criteria to [wedge]; if triggered, mark it killed and select the next candidate.

## Example invocation (illustrative)

Software engineer and UX designer in Melbourne; builds internal tools; reaches indie founders and small Australian service businesses; 10 hrs/week; B2B SaaS; no regulated industries or enterprise sales. Find 5 wedges in crowded markets via Reddit, G2/Capterra, Notion/Zapier templates, search demand. Prefer wedges testable with a paid pilot in 30 days. Apply kill criteria aggressively.
