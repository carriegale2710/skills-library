# Shrink the Decision

They don't need a perfect long-term plan. They need the smallest clear choice in front of them. Turn a forever decision into a today decision, then get them doing, because action produces information that more thinking can't.

## Step 1: Name the big decision

One line, as a question. If several decisions are tangled together, pick the one blocking the most and park the rest.

## Step 2: Name why it feels big

One line: permanence, too many options, fear of choosing wrong, identity, other people's opinions. Then split out what is truly irreversible (signing, quitting, large spend, burned bridges). Only that part deserves slow deciding; the rest is reversible and should be decided fast.

## Step 3: Shrink it

Ask: "What is the smallest version of this choice?"

| Horizon | Question |
|---|---|
| **Today** | What can I decide and act on today, in 30 minutes or less? |
| **This week** | What small, cheap experiment would show whether this direction is right? |
| **Next step** | Which one decision does that unlock next? |

Examples:
- "Should I go back to uni for a master's?" → Today: email one person who did that degree and ask if it paid off. This week: do one free lecture or short course in the subject. Next step: decide whether to apply for next intake.
- "I have 20 content ideas and can't pick." → Today: make the easiest one in under an hour and post it. This week: post 3 and see which gets a response. Next step: double down on the winner.
- "Should I buy a $2,000 camera?" → Today: borrow or rent one. This week: use it on one real shoot. Next step: buy, or don't.

## Step 4: Pick the reversible option first

Among the choices, prefer the one that is cheapest to undo, keeps the most options open and teaches the most. Even a one-way decision can usually be reached through reversible steps (conversation → trial → commitment).

## Step 5: Decide for today

Recommend one choice. Set a revisit point ("decide for now; revisit on [date] or after [event]"). If they want to keep researching, ask what specific uncertainty it would resolve. Keep researching only if the answer could change the today-choice; otherwise stop and act.

## Output

```
**Big decision:** ...
**Why it feels big:** ...
**Today:** ...
**This week:** ...
**Next step:** ...
**Revisit:** ...
```

End with "Next action: ..." naming the Today item as one physical action of 5 minutes or less.

## Rules

- Recommend one. No option menus unless asked.
- Don't solve the forever decision. If pushed, give a one-line best guess, then return to today.
- 2-3 concrete options that need weighing on evidence and criteria → hand to help-me-decide.
- High stakes (legal, medical, large financial): say so. Shrinking means smaller steps, not rushing; make "talk to the right professional" part of the next step.
- Plan only. Don't buy, send, resign or book on their behalf.
