# Reference List Builder

## When to Use This Skill

Use when the user asks for a reference list, help choosing referees, or help preparing them, or mentions: "references", "referees", "reference check".

Never put referees, references or "references available upon request" in the resume, cover letter or any other application document. Employers ask for referees separately. Build the list only when asked, as its own page.

## Choosing referees

Pick 2-3 (up to 5 if asked) who can speak to the vital criteria in the ad:

1. Recent direct manager
2. Senior colleague who saw the work
3. Cross-functional partner, client, or direct report (for leadership roles)
4. Lecturer, supervisor or volunteer lead (limited work history)

Avoid friends and family, people who barely know the user, and anyone who would be lukewarm. A referee who won't be enthusiastic is worse than none. Don't list a current employer without the user's permission.

## List format

Its own page, with the same header as the resume and the title "Referees". For each:

```
[Name]
[Title], [Organisation]
Phone: [number] | Email: [email]
Relationship: [how you worked together, dates]
```

Use the contact details the user gives. Never guess them.

## Preparing referees

1. **Ask first.** Before listing anyone, ask whether they are happy to be a referee for this role. Confirm their details.
2. **Brief them.** Send the resume, the ad, the two or three points to emphasise and the projects they might be asked about, and say when a call may come.
3. **Thank them** afterwards, whatever the outcome, and tell them the result.

Permission request:

```
Hi [Name], I'm applying for [Role] at [Company]. The role involves [one line]. Would you be happy to be a referee? I'd value your view on [project or skill].
```

Briefing email: subject "Referee for [Role] at [Company]". Include the role, what the employer is looking for, the points to emphasise, the projects and the timeframe. Attach the resume and ad.

## Special situations

- **Current employer doesn't know:** ask to delay checks until the offer stage, and offer past managers or colleagues who have left.
- **Manager has left:** list them with their new organisation.
- **Poor relationship with a manager:** use another supervisor or senior colleague from that role.
- **Little work history:** use lecturers, internship or volunteer supervisors, coaches or clients.

## Timing

Don't send referees with the application. Have the list ready for interviews and provide it when asked, usually after the final interview. Confirm each referee is available at that point.

Brief referees that they will probably be asked whether they would rehire the user, and what the user's strengths and weaker areas are.
