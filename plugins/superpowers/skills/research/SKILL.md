# Research Skill

## Goal

Gather, evaluate, and synthesize information on a topic to produce actionable insights.

## Steps

1. **Define the research question** — what exactly are you trying to learn or decide?
2. **Identify sources** — where will you find relevant information? (docs, articles, forums, experts, code)
3. **Set time budget** — how much time will you spend researching before deciding?
4. **Gather information** — collect relevant data, taking notes with source citations.
5. **Evaluate credibility** — which sources are trustworthy? Which are outdated or biased?
6. **Synthesize findings** — what patterns, contradictions, or key insights emerge?
7. **Draw conclusions** — what does this mean for your original question?
8. **Document with citations** — record findings with links/sources for future reference.

## Reference

### Leading Words

- **Research question**: The specific question your research aims to answer.
- **Time budget**: The maximum time you'll spend researching before making a decision.

### Rules

- Research without a time budget becomes procrastination. Set a limit.
- Always cite sources — future you (or reviewers) will need to verify.
- Distinguish facts from opinions in your notes.

## Common Mistakes

- **Undefined question**: Researching without a clear question leads to aimless browsing.
- **No time limit**: Research expands to fill available time. Set a budget and stick to it.
- **Source amnesia**: Forgetting where you found something. Cite as you go.
- **Analysis paralysis**: Gathering more and more information without deciding. Research → Decide → Act.

## Examples

**Bad research question:** "Learn about databases"

**Good research question:** "Which database (PostgreSQL vs MongoDB) is better for our activity feed feature with 10k writes/day?"

**Research notes format:**

```
Source: https://example.com/postgres-vs-mongo
Key point: PostgreSQL better for structured data with complex queries
Credibility: High (benchmarks with reproducible code)
```
