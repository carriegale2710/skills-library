# Summarize Skill

## Goal

Produce a concise, accurate summary that captures the essential points while omitting unnecessary detail.

## Steps

1. **Identify the purpose** — why are you summarizing this? (decision-making, reference, sharing with others?)
2. **Skim for structure** — what are the main sections, headings, or themes?
3. **Read for key points** — what are the 3-7 most important ideas or findings?
4. **Note supporting evidence** — what data, examples, or arguments support each key point?
5. **Draft the summary** — write 1-2 sentences per key point in your own words.
6. **Check accuracy** — does your summary faithfully represent the source? Any misinterpretations?
7. **Trim redundancy** — remove repetition, examples, and tangential details.
8. **Add source citation** — link to or name the original source.

## Reference

### Leading Words

- **Key point**: A central idea or finding that is essential to understanding the source.
- **Summary**: A condensed version that captures essentials while omitting detail.

### Rules

- Summaries should be 10-25% the length of the original (adjust based on purpose).
- Always attribute the source — never present summarized content as your own.
- Use your own words; don't copy phrases from the source unless quoting.

## Common Mistakes

- **Too detailed**: Including examples, edge cases, or minor points. Stick to the essentials.
- **Missing context**: Summarizing without noting key assumptions or constraints.
- **Distortion**: Misrepresenting the source's conclusions or emphasis.
- **Copy-paste**: Lifting sentences from the source instead of paraphrasing.

## Examples

**Original (200 words):** [Long article about database selection]

**Summary (40 words):**
PostgreSQL is better for structured data with complex queries and transactions. MongoDB excels at unstructured, document-based data with high write throughput. Choose PostgreSQL for financial systems; MongoDB for content management and real-time analytics.

**Source:** https://example.com/postgres-vs-mongo
