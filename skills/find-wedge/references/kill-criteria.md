# Kill Criteria and Move-on Protocol

Purpose: rules for ending a wedge early in `find-wedge`. Kill dead ideas fast; do not research a dead wedge to feel productive.

## Kill when evidence shows no

Reachable buyer, budget, urgency, switching reason, distribution path, or expansion path. Also kill on any personal "no" in `founder-profile.md` (e.g. regulated industry, enterprise sales, hardware).

## Evidence cutoffs

If a wedge fails the quality gate in `SKILL.md` (fewer than 5 independent records, or one source type only) after a time-boxed pass, mark it KILLED or parked as "insufficient evidence", and record which.

## Do not kill merely because

- The market is crowded or a large competitor exists.
- First landing-page conversion is low.
- A few people said "interesting".
- It needs more work than expected, or the wedge is narrow.

## Kill log

```
Idea / Date killed / Stage reached / Evidence reviewed
Primary kill reason / Dealbreaker rule triggered
What was learned / What to avoid next time / Possible adjacent idea
```

Example (illustrative): AI meeting-notes app for SMBs. Stage: landing-page test. Evidence: 42 Reddit posts, 28 G2 reviews, 12 Zapier templates. Reason: no switching reason, buyers use native AI notes. Avoid: categories where the incumbent bundles the core feature. Adjacent: meeting-to-CRM workflow for agencies.

## Move-on protocol

1. Mark KILLED in the decision log with the rule triggered.
2. Archive evidence; keep reusable insights.
3. Return to the candidate queue; pick the next-highest score with a different primary risk.
4. Run its cheapest test.
5. Revisit a killed idea only on new structural evidence.

A killed idea is a completed experiment, not a failure.
